# How to Beat ATS Resume Scanners

Most large employers run every resume through an **Applicant Tracking System (ATS)** before a human ever sees it. Studies suggest the majority of applications are filtered out at this automated stage — not because you're unqualified, but because your resume didn't parse cleanly.

Here's how to get past the ATS and into a recruiter's shortlist.

## How an ATS actually reads your resume

An ATS isn't "reading" in the human sense. It:

1. **Extracts text** from your file (PDF parsing is notoriously unreliable).
2. **Categorizes sections** — work history, education, skills.
3. **Scores you** against the job description's keywords and required qualifications.

If the parser can't extract your text cleanly, it doesn't matter how good your resume looks — it scores near zero.

## 1. Mirror the job description's keywords

The single highest-leverage fix is **keyword alignment**. Take the job posting and pull out:

- Role-specific nouns ("revenue operations", "Figma", "GA4")
- Action verbs they repeat ("optimize", "scale", "lead")
- Certifications and tools they name

Use the exact same terms — not synonyms. If the posting says "customer success", write "customer success", not "client relations".

## 2. Use a single-column, parser-safe layout

Skip the fancy two-column templates, text boxes, tables, and graphics. The parser reads top-to-bottom, left-to-right. Use:

- **One column**
- Standard headings (`Work Experience`, `Education`, `Skills`)
- A standard font (Arial, Calibri, Georgia)
- `.docx` or plain PDF (never a scanned image)

## 3. Structure every role as action → result

For each position, use the format: **strong verb + what you did + measurable result**.

> "Optimized onboarding flow, reducing time-to-first-value by 40%."

Numbers make you rank higher and give the recruiter proof.

## 4. Avoid the parse-killers

- Headers/footers with your contact info (often ignored by parsers)
- Tables, columns, images, charts
- Unusual fonts or excessive styling
- Acronyms without the full term ("KPI (Key Performance Indicator)")

## 5. Put skills in a dedicated, keyword-rich section

A plain `Skills` list makes it trivial for the ATS to match requirements. Group them: `Marketing: SEO, GA4, A/B testing`.

**The short version:** a resume that mirrors the job description, uses clean formatting, and quantifies results will beat the ATS more often than a "designed" resume that scores zero.
