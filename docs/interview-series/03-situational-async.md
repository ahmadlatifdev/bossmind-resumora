# Script 3 — Situational & Asynchronous Interview Strategy

**Duration:** 8 minutes | **Word count:** ~1,200 | **Language:** English

---

## [0:00–0:30] HOOK

The interview is changing. More companies now send you a prompt and a camera — no interviewer, no follow-up questions, no second chance. You get two to six minutes. What you say in that window decides whether you advance. In the next eight minutes, I will show you how to open strong, think clearly under time pressure, and close with command.

## [0:30–2:00] SEGMENT 1 — The Asynchronous Format

Three types. One — video prompt with a fixed timer. Two — written response with a deadline. Three — recorded presentation with slides.

Each one shares the same constraint: no interviewer to read. Your energy, clarity, and structure must carry the entire message. There is no room to gauge feedback and adjust.

The candidate who wins asynchronous interviews is not the smartest. It is the one who structures the fastest.

## [2:00–4:00] SEGMENT 2 — The 30-90-30 Framework

Structure every async response in three blocks. Thirty seconds to frame. Ninety seconds to deliver. Thirty seconds to close.

30 seconds to frame: Open by restating the question in your own words. "You are asking how I would handle a product launch that is losing momentum. Here is my approach." This signals that you understood the prompt.

90 seconds to deliver: Give three points. Not five. Not one. Three. Each with a concrete example or action. Structure beats depth under time pressure.

30 seconds to close: End with a single sentence that summarizes your position. "That is how I would stabilize the launch — diagnose, prioritize, communicate." Do not trail off. Land the plane.

## [4:00–6:00] SEGMENT 3 — Critical Thinking Under Time Pressure

The interviewer is not checking whether you have the right answer. They are checking whether you can think in a structured way when the clock is running.

Three techniques. One — name the trade-off. "The fastest path is X. The safer path is Y. I would choose Y because the risk of X is unrecoverable." Two — state your assumptions. "I am assuming the team has bandwidth to reprioritize. If not, my answer changes." Three — acknowledge what you do not know. "I do not have data on churn this quarter. My first action would be to find it."

The candidates who look weak in async interviews are the ones who pretend to know everything. The ones who look strong are the ones who think transparently.

## [6:00–7:30] SEGMENT 4 — EQ and Tone in a Camera-Only Room

You are speaking to a lens, not a person. This changes everything. Your face must carry the warmth that your voice would carry in person. Slow down. Pause between points. Smile when you open.

The most common mistake is rushing. When there is no feedback, we speak faster to fill the silence. Fight this. Speak at eighty percent of your normal pace. The extra twenty percent is what makes you sound thoughtful instead of nervous.

If you are recording a video response, do three takes. The first is a warm-up. The second is the real one. The third is the insurance. Choose the second or third.

## [7:30–8:00] CLOSING RECAP

Three things. One — structure: thirty seconds to frame, ninety to deliver, thirty to close. Two — think transparently: name trade-offs, state assumptions, admit unknowns. Three — slow down; the camera rewards composure.

In the final video, I will show you how to navigate global interview norms — India, US, UK, Canada, Australia — and how to position yourself for a multi-market career. See you there.

## Production Notes

- Camera framing: chest-up, eyes in upper third of frame
- Pacing: ~150 words per minute, slightly slower than usual
- Tone: composed, thoughtful, confident
- B-roll: countdown timer, split-screen of good/bad async response, on-screen 30-90-30 diagram
- Lower-third graphics: 30-90-30 framework, three-point structure
