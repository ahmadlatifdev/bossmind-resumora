# Episode 6 — Resumora Walkthrough

**Topic:** A guided tour of the Resumora platform for building and improving a resume.
**Runtime:** ~8–10 minutes.

[Scene: A clean, modern screen showing the Resumora dashboard. The Career Coach sits beside the Job Seeker, both looking at the screen.]

Coach: Now that we've covered the strategy, let me walk you through Resumora itself. This is the tool where you'll actually build and refine your resume.

Job Seeker: I've been writing everything in a word processor, so I'm curious what makes this different.

[Scene: Close up on the screen as the cursor hovers over the "Create Resume" button.]

Coach: The big difference is that Resumora guides the process. Instead of staring at a blank page, you answer prompts, and it helps you turn those answers into strong, structured bullet points.

Job Seeker: Where do I even start? Do I need to write the whole thing from scratch?

[Scene: Screen share of the "Start from scratch" and "Upload existing resume" options side by side.]

Coach: You have two paths. You can start fresh, or you can upload a resume you already have. If you upload one, Resumora reads it and helps you improve what's there. Most people find that easier.

Job Seeker: Let's say I start fresh. What's the first screen I'll see?

[Scene: The cursor clicks "Create Resume," opening a form with fields for name, title, and contact info.]

Coach: You'll fill in your basics first — name, target job title, and contact details. Simple, but it sets up everything else, because the tool tailors its suggestions to the role you're aiming for.

Job Seeker: What about the experience section? That's where I usually get stuck.

[Scene: Screen share of the experience form, with a prompt asking "What did you do in this role?"]

Coach: Here's where the guidance kicks in. For each role, it asks what you did, and then it suggests stronger, achievement-focused versions of your bullets. You still control the wording, but you're not starting from zero.

Job Seeker: So it's like a coach giving me suggestions in real time?

[Scene: The Coach smiles and nods.]

Coach: That's a good way to think about it. It suggests stronger verbs and prompts you to add outcomes, but every word stays yours. Nothing is generated that you didn't approve.

Job Seeker: Does it help with the summary and skills sections too?

[Scene: Screen share scrolling through the summary and skills sections, each with helpful prompts.]

Coach: Yes. It helps you write a focused summary and organize your skills by relevance to your target role. It also flags common weak phrases and suggests alternatives.

Job Seeker: What about the design? I've seen resumes that look cluttered.

[Scene: The screen shows several clean, professional templates in a gallery.]

Coach: Resumora gives you clean, ATS-friendly templates. You pick the look, and the content stays structured. That matters because a clean layout keeps both software and human readers from getting lost.

Job Seeker: Wait — ATS. Does the formatting actually matter for those systems?

[Scene: Close up on the Coach pointing at the template options.]

Coach: It does. Fancy graphics, tables, and text boxes can confuse automated screening. Resumora's templates are designed to stay readable, so your content actually gets parsed correctly.

Job Seeker: Can I tailor the same resume for different jobs, like we talked about last time?

[Scene: Screen share of a "Save as new version" option appearing.]

Coach: Absolutely. You can save multiple versions for different roles. That way you can tailor each application without losing your master copy. It fits perfectly with the tailoring strategy we covered.

Job Seeker: Does it offer anything for the cover letter too?

[Scene: The screen navigates to a cover letter builder with prompts.]

Coach: It does. It helps you build a cover letter using the same information from your resume, so the two stay consistent. You'll still personalize it for each job, but the structure is there.

Job Seeker: What about the interview side? I remember you mentioned the coaching features.

[Scene: The screen shows a menu with "Interview Prep" and "Practice Questions" sections.]

Coach: There's an interview prep section with common questions and guidance for answering them. It ties back to the STAR method we discussed. It's a place to practice before the real thing.

Job Seeker: That's helpful. How do I get my resume into a PDF I can send?

[Scene: The cursor clicks "Export," and a clean PDF preview appears.]

Coach: When you're done, you export it as a PDF. That's the standard format employers expect. The export keeps your formatting clean so it looks the same on their end.

Job Seeker: What if I get stuck on a section and don't know what to write?

[Scene: The Coach points to a help icon on the screen.]

Coach: Every section has prompts and examples to guide you. And the suggestions it gives are a starting point — you're always the one deciding what stays. The tool assists, but you stay in control.

Job Seeker: That's reassuring. It sounds like it removes the friction without doing the thinking for me.

[Scene: The Coach nods and leans back.]

Coach: That's exactly the right framing. Resumora helps you present yourself more clearly and more quickly, but the substance comes from you. And remember — no tool can promise you a job. What it can do is help you put your best, most honest foot forward.

Job Seeker: That's a realistic way to look at it. I feel ready to actually start building.

[Scene: The Coach smiles and gestures toward the screen.]

Coach: Then let's do it. You have the strategy, and now you have the tool. Start with your most recent role, and we'll go from there.

[Scene: The Job Seeker hovers over the screen, then asks another practical question.]

Job Seeker: Does Resumora save my work automatically, or do I need to save manually?

[Scene: Screen share of an autosave indicator appearing briefly in the corner.]

Coach: It saves automatically as you go, so you don't have to worry about losing your progress. You can also save named versions, which is handy when you're tailoring for different jobs.

Job Seeker: Can I come back and edit my resume later, after I've learned more from this process?

[Scene: The Coach nods and points to the "My Resumes" menu.]

Coach: Of course. Everything you build is stored in your account, so you can return, revise, and improve it anytime. A resume is a living document, not a one-time task.

Job Seeker: Does the platform support multiple languages? I might apply to roles that want French or Spanish.

[Scene: Screen share of a language selector showing English, French, and Spanish.]

Coach: Yes, it supports English, French, and Spanish, which is useful if you're targeting markets where those languages matter. You can switch the language of the interface and your resume content.

Job Seeker: Who can see my resume? Is my data private?

[Scene: The Coach turns to face the Job Seeker directly, speaking seriously.]

Coach: Your information stays yours. The platform is built to keep your resume private — it isn't shared or published anywhere without your action. You control what you export and where you send it.

Job Seeker: That's important to me. Is there a way to get help if I get stuck on a specific section?

[Scene: The Coach points to a help and examples menu on the screen.]

Coach: Every section has built-in examples and guidance. And the prompts we've talked about — like action verbs and achievement framing — are baked right into the suggestions, so help is always a click away.

Job Seeker: This really does bring the whole strategy together in one place. I feel like I finally have a clear path forward.

[Scene: The Coach smiles warmly and gestures toward the screen one last time.]

Coach: That's exactly what we wanted. The strategy gives you the why, and the tool gives you the how. Together they help you present your honest best — nothing more, nothing less — and let the results follow from there.
